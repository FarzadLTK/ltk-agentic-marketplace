# Five-minute demo script

Open `index.html?speed=0.35`. Leave the default budget ($1,000) and brief.

## 0:00 — Framing (before clicking anything)

"Personal agents are about to shop for consumers and negotiate for businesses. Sierra, Meta, Shopify, Stripe, Walmart, Target and OpenAI are standardizing how they talk to companies right now. This is what LTK looks like when every creator and every brand has an agent, and the creator never logs in."

Point at the header: Brand view, Creator view, speed control. Point at the yellow pill: everything is simulated.

## 0:30 — Deploy

Click **Deploy budget**.

Scene 1: "The brand agent turns a sentence into a contract it can enforce: budget, audience, channels, reel required, 10-day deadline, max 60% to one creator, walk-away threshold."

## 1:00 — Creator agents respond

"Four creator agents answer in seconds. Each one knows its own audience, how well it has sold this brand before, what's already in its content bank, and what its human's time is worth. Notice the tags: most deliverables come from the bank, a few need the human."

Point at Maren: large audience, low affinity, high floors. "Watch what happens to her."

## 1:45 — Negotiation

"Four negotiations run in parallel. Every offer is a typed object, not a chat message." Point at the first red referee card. "The LTK referee blocks any offer below a creator's floor before the creator agent even sees it. That single rule removes the failure mode where a weak agent signs a money-losing deal."

Point at Juniper's thread: "High affinity, so the brand agent pays her in upside: lower flat, plus two points of commission. Her agent credits that at a discount and closes in two rounds."

Point at Maren's thread: "Her single Instagram post floors at $350 against a $163 allocation. The brand agent walks, logs why, and both agents will re-engage automatically on the next brief that fits."

Point at Sofia's thread: "When the brand hits its ceiling and the gap is small, her agent closes instead of dragging it out."

## 3:00 — Settlement

"$1,000 became three deals and a performance pool. Each card shows the floor check, the share check, three signatures, escrow terms, and the attribution links that were minted."

## 3:30 — Execution

"Here is the part that matters for creators. Bank assets are scheduled with no human involvement. New content triggers one lock-screen ask with the math on it: fee plus projected commission minus her time at her hourly floor. The agent only asks when the answer is yes."

Point at Theo: "Two new stories, one batched ask, 48 minutes."

## 4:15 — Money

"Thirty days compressed. Flat fees release on go-live. Commission lands per order, not per campaign. Some orders are flagged as checkout by the shopper's personal agent with a verified credential; that's the attribution problem solved for the agent era. ROAS updates live. The bonus pool releases to the top converter."

## 4:45 — Creator view

Click **Creator view**. "Juniper's phone. She earned over $1,100 and spent 150 minutes, all of it shooting one reel. She also didn't have to say no to Harbor Denim; her agent did. She set this policy once."

## Close

"What's real here is the engine: allocation, package sizing, referee, patience-based bargaining, EV gating. What's fake is the data. Swapping in real creators from the LTK data lake is a data-layer change. Exposing these agents through MCP into ChatGPT and Glean, and through Poppy to external personal agents, is the roadmap."

## Variations to show on request

- Set budget to $500 and redeploy: more walk-aways, smaller packages.
- Remove "at least one reel" from the brief: Juniper's package reshapes around bank assets.
- Raise the budget to $2,500: Maren's allocation clears her $350 floor and she gets a deal.
- Switch speed to Instant and show the full page at once.
